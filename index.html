Project Overview

This project evaluates the aerodynamic behaviour of a compact axial cooling fan commonly used in CPU thermal solutions. The goal is to quantify velocity magnitude distribution and static pressure field around the rotating blades.

Simulation Results

Contour plots extracted from a mid-plane slice through the fan axis. Colour scales reveal the high-velocity regions near the blade tips and the pressure recovery zones.

Velocity Magnitude (m/s)

Peak velocities concentrate at the blade trailing edges and tip regions. The flow field shows a clean, axisymmetric pattern with rapid decay outside the rotor plane — desirable for focused cooling of a heat sink.

Static Pressure (Pa)

Low-pressure zones form on the suction side of each blade, while higher pressure appears on the pressure side. The radial pressure gradient indicates effective work input by the rotor into the fluid.

Key Observations

  • Velocity magnitude reaches approximately 2 m/s near the blade tips under the simulated operating condition.
  • The pressure field exhibits classic axial-fan behaviour: suction on the upstream face and pressure rise downstream.
  • Flow remains largely attached; no major separation bubbles are visible in the mid-plane slice.
  • The circular domain and symmetry confirm that the mesh and boundary conditions produce a physically consistent solution.

Methodology

A structured CFD workflow was followed from CAD import through post-processing.

1. Geometry & Domain

5-blade axial fan CAD model imported (STEP). A cylindrical fluid domain was created around the rotor with sufficient radial and axial extent to capture near-field aerodynamics without wall interference.

2. Meshing

Hybrid mesh with refined prism layers near blade surfaces to resolve the boundary layer. Local refinement applied in the tip-gap and trailing-edge regions for accurate velocity gradients.

3. Physics Setup

Steady-state RANS simulation. Incompressible air at standard conditions. Rotating reference frame (or sliding mesh equivalent) applied to the rotor zone. Appropriate turbulence model selected for internal flow with moderate Reynolds number.

4. Boundary Conditions

Inlet: atmospheric pressure / velocity inlet. Outlet: pressure outlet. Rotor walls treated as no-slip moving walls. Outer domain walls: symmetry or far-field.

5. Post-Processing

Mid-plane slices extracted for velocity magnitude and static pressure. Contours visualised with consistent colour maps to highlight aerodynamic features.
