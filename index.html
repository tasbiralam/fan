Hand Calculation vs CFD — Analysis

  • Difference: 19.7% — Hand calculation over-predicts flow rate relative to CFD.
  • Similarity: Both methods show the same order of magnitude (~0.04 m³/s) and confirm turbulent regime at the tip.
  • Why the gap exists: The hand formula assumes uniform velocity across the entire swept disk and applies a single efficiency factor (η = 0.5). CFD resolves the real blade geometry, tip leakage, hub blockage, boundary layers and non-uniform velocity profile — all of which reduce the net delivered flow.
  • Mass conservation: Inlet and outlet CFD flows differ by only 1.88%, indicating a well-converged, physically consistent simulation.
  • Engineering takeaway: A simple analytical estimate is useful for first-order sizing; CFD is required for accurate performance prediction and design refinement.
Inlet and Outlet boundaries were set to fixed Pressure = 0 Pa (open atmospheric condition). Domain-average pressure is therefore not a meaningful output; the local pressure difference across each blade is what reflects the fan’s aerodynamic loading.

CFD Result Visuals

Mid-plane contour slices extracted from the COMSOL solution.

Velocity Magnitude (m/s)

Highest at the blade tip. Velocity scales with radius (v = ω × r). The tip moves fastest and shows the peak velocity; near the hub and far from the rotor the velocity drops toward zero.

Static Pressure (Pa)

Differs across each blade face. Pressure side pushes air (higher pressure); suction side pulls air along (lower pressure). This difference generates the thrust — the same principle as lift on a wing.
